The plasma membrane is a fluid, selectively permeable phospholipid bilayer that separates the cell's interior from its environment. It controls which molecules can enter or leave the cell — small nonpolar molecules such as oxygen and carbon dioxide pass through freely, while larger or charged particles need help from transport proteins, sometimes using ATP for active transport against a concentration gradient. Embedded proteins also serve as receptors that trigger signal transduction pathways, and cholesterol within the bilayer keeps the membrane fluid and structurally stable across different temperatures.
